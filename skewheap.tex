\title[Skew hook formula and simple-spectrum heaps]{The skew hook formula does
not extend to simple-spectrum heaps}

\author{Jaideep Sai Padhi}
\address{Department of Computer Science, Purdue University}
\email{jpadhi@purdue.edu}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
The coloured posets arising from simple-spectrum torus fixed points
\cite[\S5.4.2]{DKK} are hook length posets but need not be $d$-complete.  We
show that the skew hook formula of Naruse--Okada \cite[Thm.~1.2]{NO}, read with
its own hook function, does not extend to them.  The obstruction is the hook
function rather than the sum over excited diagrams, and it is already present
at $F=\varnothing$.  The witness has eight elements and the verification is
finite: at most eight cases.  Since the poset is a hook length poset, the
$F=\varnothing$ identity does hold for it with the correct hook function, so
only the recipe for $h$ is at fault; we state the resulting question.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\section{The question and the answer}

Let $\Gamma$ be a simply-laced Dynkin diagram with Weyl group $W$, and let
$w\in W$ have reduced word $s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_\ell}$.  Its \emph{heap}
\cite[Def.~5.2]{DKK} is the poset on $\{1,\dots,\ell\}$ generated by the
relations between positions carrying equal or adjacent colours, each element
$a$ coloured by $i_a$.  For $\lambda$-minuscule $w$ the heap is $d$-complete
and the Peterson--Proctor formula \cite[Thm.~1.1]{NO} evaluates its
reverse-plane-partition series.  Dinkins, Karpov and Krylov observe
\cite[\S5.4.2]{DKK} that the hypothesis actually needed is weaker --- that the
torus act on each $V_i$ with \emph{simple spectrum} --- and ask for a
combinatorial characterisation of such $w$.  Each resulting poset $P=P(w)$
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hook}
   \sum_{\varphi\in\rpp(P)}q^{|\varphi|}
   \;=\;\prod_{u\in P}\bigl(1-q^{\,h(u)}\bigr)^{-1}
\end{equation}
for some $h:P\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$, that is, is a \emph{hook length poset} in the
sense of \cite{IT}; the class strictly contains the $d$-complete posets.
Nothing below depends on that general statement, since \eqref{eq:hook} is
verified directly for the one poset we use.

The following was asked of the author by V.~Krylov in October 2026.

\begin{question}\label{q:main}
Naruse and Okada prove a \emph{skew} hook formula \cite[Thm.~1.2]{NO}: for
$d$-complete $P$ and an order ideal $F\subseteq P$, the series of reverse plane
partitions on $P\setminus F$ is a finite sum over the excited diagrams of $F$
in $P$, with hook function \cite[Def.~2.6]{NO}.  Does it extend to the
skew-heaps $P\setminus F$ of the simple-spectrum setting, where $P$ is a hook
length poset but need not be $d$-complete?
\end{question}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
No.  There is an eight-element heap in $D_4$ which is a hook length poset, is
not $d$-complete, and for which \eqref{eq:hook} fails for every hook function
\cite[Def.~2.6]{NO} produces.  Hence \cite[Thm.~1.2]{NO}, read with its own
hook function, already fails there at $F=\varnothing$.
\end{theorem}

Only the degenerate case of the formula is needed.  When $F=\varnothing$ the
excited diagrams reduce to the empty one and the set of excited peaks is empty,
so the formula becomes \eqref{eq:hook}; this is the specialisation DKK quote at
the end of their \S5.3.  So it suffices to rule out every admissible $h$, and
the next lemma makes that a question about multisets, hence finite.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:unique}
If $\prod_{i}(1-q^{a_i})^{-1}=\prod_{j}(1-q^{b_j})^{-1}$ in $\mathbb{Z}[[q]]$
for finite multisets of positive integers, the multisets coincide.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $c_d$ be the multiplicity of $d$ among the $a_i$.  Then
\[
  \log\prod_i(1-q^{a_i})^{-1}=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac1m\sum_iq^{a_im}
  =\sum_{N\ge1}q^N\sum_{d\mid N}c_d\,\frac dN ,
\]
so $N[q^N]\log(\cdot)=\sum_{d\mid N}d\,c_d$ for all $N$.  These values
determine $d\,c_d$, hence $c_d$, by induction on $N$.
\end{proof}

We may therefore speak of the \emph{true hook multiset} of a hook length
poset: it is determined by the series alone.

\section{The witness}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:witness}
Take $\Gamma=D_4$ in the Bourbaki labelling, so vertex $2$ is trivalent with
neighbours $1,3,4$, and let
\[
   w=s_2s_1s_2s_3s_4s_2s_1s_3 ,
\]
reduced of length $8$.  Its heap $P$ is
\[
  p_1\prec p_2\prec p_3\prec\{p_4,p_5\}\prec p_6\prec\{p_7,p_8\},
  \qquad
  \text{colours } 2,1,2,3,4,2,1,3,
\]
with $p_4,p_5$ incomparable \textup{(}colours $3,4$ non-adjacent\textup{)} and
likewise $p_7,p_8$ \textup{(}colours $1,3$\textup{)}.  Then $P$ is not
$d$-complete, and its true hook multiset is $\{1,1,3,4,4,5,6,7\}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The order is the transitive closure of the heap relations read off the word.
For $d$-completeness no computation is needed: $p_7,p_8$ are incomparable with
common lower cover $p_6$, so they span a $d_3^-$-interval, which Proctor's
axiom at $k=3$ requires to have a top; but both are maximal in $P$.  The dual
poset is not $d$-complete either, so this is independent of orientation.

For the multiset, both sides of \eqref{eq:hook} are computed independently and
compared to order $q^{26}$.  A reverse plane partition
$\varphi:P\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}$, weakly increasing upwards, is equivalent to
the decreasing chain of order filters $F_k=\{x:\varphi(x)\ge k\}$ with
$|\varphi|=\sum_k|F_k|$; repetitions $F_k=F_{k+1}$ are allowed, so
\[
  \sum_{\varphi\in\rpp(P)}q^{|\varphi|}=1+\sum_{\varnothing\ne F}g(F),
  \qquad
  g(F)=\frac{q^{|F|}}{1-q^{|F|}}
       \Bigl(1+\sum_{\varnothing\ne G\subsetneq F}g(G)\Bigr).
\]
$P$ has ten nonempty filters, and the recursion gives
\[
  1+2q+3q^2+5q^3+9q^4+14q^5+21q^6+31q^7+45q^8+63q^9+\cdots,
\]
agreeing with $\prod_{h\in\{1,1,3,4,4,5,6,7\}}(1-q^h)^{-1}$ in every
coefficient to $q^{26}$; Lemma \ref{lem:unique} does the rest.  The first two
coefficients check by hand: $[q^1]=2$ counts the maximal elements $p_7,p_8$,
and $[q^2]=3$ counts $2\cdot\mathbf 1_{p_7}$, $2\cdot\mathbf 1_{p_8}$ and
$\mathbf 1_{p_7}+\mathbf 1_{p_8}$.
\end{proof}

Off the $d$-complete locus \cite[Def.~2.6]{NO} does not define a single
function.  An element may top several $d_k$-intervals, and at each such element
the recursion may use any one of them, or the order-ideal count
$\#\{v:v\preceq u\}$ instead; so it defines a family.  We exhaust it.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:exhaust}
For $P$ as above, three elements top a $d_k$-interval in the orientation of
Proposition \ref{prop:witness}, giving eight choices and six distinct
multisets,
\[
  \{1,2,3,4,4,5,6,6\},\ \{1,2,3,4,4,5,6,7\},\ \{1,2,3,4,4,5,7,7\},
\]
\[
  \{1,2,3,4,4,6,6,6\},\ \{1,2,3,4,4,6,6,7\},\ \{1,2,3,4,4,6,7,7\};
\]
in the dual orientation --- the corner-as-maximum convention of
\cite[Def.~2.6]{NO} --- two elements do, giving six choices and two multisets,
$\{1,1,3,4,4,5,7,8\}$ and $\{1,1,3,4,4,6,7,8\}$.  In neither case does
$\{1,1,3,4,4,5,6,7\}$ occur.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Finite enumeration, eight and six cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main}]
If \cite[Thm.~1.2]{NO} held for $P$ with hook function $h$ from
\cite[Def.~2.6]{NO}, then $F=\varnothing$ would give \eqref{eq:hook} for $h$,
so by Lemma \ref{lem:unique} the multiset of $h$ would be the true hook
multiset $\{1,1,3,4,4,5,6,7\}$ of Proposition \ref{prop:witness}.  Proposition
\ref{prop:exhaust} rules that out.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Both orientations are recorded because the hook function is pinned where the
series is a hook product while \cite[Def.~2.6]{NO} is stated in the dual
convention, and the conclusion should not rest on that correspondence.  In the
first orientation the failure is narrow, $\{1,2,3,4,4,5,6,7\}$ differing in one
entry; in the second the two largest hooks come out $7,8$ against $6,7$, so the
product has the wrong order of vanishing at $q\to1$.
\end{remark}

\section{What this does and does not rule out}

What fails is \cite[Thm.~1.2]{NO} \emph{read with its own hook function}, on
hook length heaps that are not $d$-complete, and it fails at $F=\varnothing$,
before excited diagrams play any role.  What does \emph{not} fail is
\eqref{eq:hook}: the witness is a hook length poset, so the $F=\varnothing$
case of the formula is true for it with the hook function realising
$\{1,1,3,4,4,5,6,7\}$.  Nothing here bears on $d$-complete $P$, where
\cite[Thm.~1.2]{NO} is a theorem, nor on any formula whose hook function is
defined otherwise.  So it is the recipe for $h$ that is at fault, not the shape
of the formula, and the natural question survives.

\begin{question}\label{q:new}
Every simple-spectrum heap $P$ carries a hook function $h_P$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:hook}, unique as a multiset by Lemma \ref{lem:unique}.  Is there a
recursion computing $h_P$, agreeing with \cite[Def.~2.6]{NO} on the
$d$-complete locus, for which \cite[Thm.~1.2]{NO} holds throughout this class?
\end{question}

For how typical the witness is: over all reduced words of length at most $8$ in
$D_4$, among the heaps that are hook length posets, the $d$-complete ones reach
their true hook multiset in all $336$ cases and the non-$d$-complete ones fail
to in all $36$.  In this range the dichotomy is exactly $d$-completeness.  All
$36$ failures have $|P|=8$, so the witness is one of $36$ of minimal size.

Every claim above is a finite check.  The scripts are in the
\texttt{skewheap} directory of
\url{https://github.com/jaideepsaipadhi/dkk-simple-spectrum}:
\verb|certify2.py| computes both sides of \eqref{eq:hook} for the witness in
either orientation, from scratch and sharing no code with the rest of the
project; \verb|orient.py| exhausts the \cite[Def.~2.6]{NO} family and prints
every reachable multiset; \verb|minwit.py 8 D4| recovers the witness as a
smallest one; \verb|reach.py 8 11 D4| produces the $336$ and $36$.
